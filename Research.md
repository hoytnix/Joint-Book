Analytical Cross-Check and Chemical Plausibility Assessment of Paper Thin-Layer Chromatography Migration Windows in the Purple Canary ProtocolPlanar chromatography conducted on untreated cellulose substrates using lipophilic hydrocarbon mobile phases represents a distinct analytical domain compared to conventional forensic toxicology workflows. Standard forensic laboratory protocols for the presumptive and semi-quantitative analysis of cannabinoids and seized drugs rely almost exclusively on high-performance thin-layer chromatography (HPTLC) performed on inorganic silica gel $60\text{ F}_{254}$ layers. These compendial systems deploy toxic, volatile developing solvents such as petroleum ether, diethyl ether, toluene, and halogenated hydrocarbons, coupled with carcinogenic diazonium visualization reagents including Fast Blue B or Fast Blue BB.The Purple Canary Protocol adapts planar separation to point-of-care harm reduction by replacing hazardous laboratory reagents with an environmentally benign, food-grade system consisting of Whatman Grade 1 cellulose paper as the stationary phase and a binary solution of $95.5\%$ D-limonene and $0.5\%$ curcumin as both the mobile phase and integrated fluorophore indicator. Within this operational framework, empirical retention factor ($R_f$) migration windows have been established for eight target compounds across four toxicological classes:$$\text{fentanyl freebase }(0.12\text{--}0.18) < \text{cannabinol [CBN] }(0.34\text{--}0.40) < \text{cannabidiol [CBD] }(0.42\text{--}0.48) < \Delta^9\text{-tetrahydrocannabinol [THC] }(0.52\text{--}0.58)$$$$< \text{ADB-PINACA }(0.68\text{--}0.74) < \text{MDMB-4en-PINACA }(0.72\text{--}0.78) < \text{medium-chain triglycerides [MCT] }(0.80\text{--}0.88) < \text{vitamin E acetate }(0.84\text{--}0.92)$$[cite: 1]Evaluating the plausibility of these empirical migration windows requires translating classical retention mechanisms governed by the acidic silanol adsorption matrix of silica gel into the partition-and-adsorption mechanics of semi-crystalline poly-hydroxyl cellulose developed with a terpene solvent.Compound-by-Compound Chromatographic Reference DataDirect literature values for paper chromatography developed with terpene hydrocarbons are virtually non-existent because official forensic methodology transitioned away from paper chromatography toward silica TLC by the late 1960s, prior to the emergence of synthetic cannabinoids and contemporary vape cutting agents. Anal., 22:421–427 (2009); Sherma & Rabel (2019).Chemical Plausibility Assessment of the Migration SequenceThe relative migration velocity of an organic solute in planar chromatography is governed by the equilibrium partition coefficient between the moving mobile phase and the stationary bed, molecular polar surface area (PSA), hydrogen-bond donor and acceptor counts, and localized electronic interactions. In a normal-phase system consisting of cellulose and D-limonene, more polar, hydrogen-bonding species undergo preferential retention on the polysaccharide network, whereas lipophilic, non-polar species partition into the migrating hydrocarbon front.Polar Retention and Baseline Confinement of Fentanyl FreebaseThe placement of fentanyl freebase within the lowest retention corridor ($R_f = 0.12 - 0.18$) is chemically sound and fully consistent with normal-phase fundamentals. Fentanyl contains a basic tertiary piperidine nitrogen ($\text{p}K_a \approx 8.4$) alongside an amide carbonyl group. Whatman Grade 1 paper consists of pure $\beta$-(1,4)-D-glucan polysaccharide chains presenting high concentrations of primary and secondary hydroxyl functionalities, typically retaining between $5\%$ and $8\%$ bound atmospheric water within its amorphous domains.D-Limonene is a pure hydrocarbon terpene possessing a low dielectric constant ($\varepsilon \approx 2.3$) and zero hydrogen-bonding capability. In classical forensic silica systems, resolving basic amine alkaloids requires the addition of strong volatile bases such as diethylamine or concentrated ammonia to suppress silanol ionization and prevent baseline streaking. In the absence of an alkaline modifier, the tertiary piperidine core of fentanyl engages in strong dipole-dipole interactions, hydrogen bonding, and ion-dipole attractions with the cellulose hydroxyl matrix and its hydration layer. Because non-polar D-limonene possesses negligible eluotropic strength for basic, polar amines, fentanyl is chromatographically retarded and remains arrested near the baseline origin.Partition Mechanics of the Phytocannabinoid TriadIn traditional silica TLC utilizing non-polar alkane/ether mixtures, the standard elution order is cannabidiol ($R_f \approx 0.35$), followed by cannabinol ($R_f \approx 0.45$), and $\Delta^9$-tetrahydrocannabinol ($R_f \approx 0.52$). Silica retention is dominated by adsorption to acidic surface silanols: CBD contains two free phenolic hydroxyl groups on an open resorcinol ring, resulting in stronger surface binding than THC and CBN, which each possess only a single phenolic hydroxyl group.In the Purple Canary system, the measured retention order demonstrates a distinct inversion between CBN and CBD:$$\text{CBN } (0.34\text{--}0.40) < \text{CBD } (0.42\text{--}0.48) < \Delta^9\text{-THC } (0.52\text{--}0.58)$$[cite: 1]This migration sequence is chemically plausible when evaluated under the specific stationary-phase properties of cellulose and the molecular structure of D-limonene. Unlike the rigid, three-dimensional silanol network of amorphous silica, cellulose chromatography operates via a combined partition and adsorption mechanism where planar aromatic surfaces can undergo stable non-covalent adsorption and steric accommodation against the flat pyranose rings of crystalline cellulose microfibrils. Cannabinol represents the fully aromatized, planar end-product of THC degradation. Its tricyclic dibenzopyran core is planar and rigid, maximizing contact surface area with the cellulose sheet and enhancing stationary-phase retardation.Conversely, the chemical structure of cannabidiol comprises an open resorcinol ring attached to a $p$-mentha-1,8-dienyl monoterpene substituent. D-Limonene is itself a cyclic $p$-menthadiene hydrocarbon. This structural homology facilitates preferential solvation of the terpene moiety of CBD by the D-limonene mobile phase, enhancing its partition out of the aqueous-cellulose stationary layer and driving it higher up the paper than would occur on silica.This relative order finds historical support in forensic literature: under Clarke’s System TAH (silica gel G developed with benzene : dioxane : acetic acid), CBN similarly exhibits a lower mobility ($R_f = 0.45$) than CBD ($R_f = 0.60$). Finally, $\Delta^9$-THC combines high lipophilicity ($\log P \approx 6.97$) with a partially saturated cyclohexenyl ring and a single phenolic hydroxyl group, minimizing hydrogen-bonding interactions with cellulose while partitioning into the lipophilic mobile phase to yield an intermediate $R_f$ of $0.52 - 0.58$.Structural Discrimination of Synthetic Cannabinoid Receptor AgonistsThe empirical windows assigned to the synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists ADB-PINACA ($0.68 - 0.74$) and MDMB-4en-PINACA ($0.72 - 0.78$) place both compounds distinctly above the natural phytocannabinoid corridor while maintaining reproducible separation between the two synthetics.This migration behavior corresponds directly to functional group differences. Neither ADB-PINACA nor MDMB-4en-PINACA possesses phenolic hydroxyl groups, which are the primary determinants of phytocannabinoid retention on polar substrates. Their polar functionalities are confined to an indazole core, an amide carbonyl linker, and either a terminal carboxamide or ester function. Because they lack hydrogen-bond donating phenolic rings, their overall affinity for cellulose paper is significantly lower than that of THC, CBD, or CBN, causing them to migrate farther toward the solvent front.The separation between the two synthetic agonists reflects classical structure-retention relationships. ADB-PINACA features a terminal tert-leucinamide group terminating in a primary carboxamide ($-\text{CONH}_2$). Primary amides possess two hydrogen-bond donor protons alongside a strongly polarized carbonyl acceptor, creating interaction sites for the cellulose hydroxyl network. In contrast, MDMB-4en-PINACA possesses a terminal methyl ester ($-\text{COOCH}_3$) on its tert-leucinate core. Esters have zero hydrogen-bond donor capacity and act merely as weak hydrogen-bond acceptors.Furthermore, the 4-pentenyl tail of MDMB-4en-PINACA incorporates a terminal olefinic double bond, which increases its polarizability and miscibility with the unsaturated terpene solvent matrix. The absence of hydrogen-donating protons in the ester linkage ensures that MDMB-4en-PINACA experiences less stationary-phase drag than ADB-PINACA, confirming the observed sequence where MDMB-4en-PINACA elutes slightly ahead of its carboxamide analog.Front-Zone Migration of Lipophilic Cutting AgentsThe upper migration registers are occupied by medium-chain triglycerides ($0.80 - 0.88$) and vitamin E acetate ($0.84 - 0.92$). Their placement near the solvent front is fully consistent with lipid chromatographic principles.Medium-chain triglycerides (MCT oils) consist of fully esterified glycerol backbones linked to $C_8$ (caprylic) and $C_{10}$ (capric) saturated fatty acid chains. Triacylglycerols are neutral lipids characterized by calculated $\log P$ values exceeding $8.0$ and complete miscibility with non-polar terpene solvents. They possess no hydroxyl, amine, or carboxylic acid functional groups. The three ester dipoles on the glycerol backbone interact weakly with cellulose, allowing the bulk non-polar hydrocarbon chains to carry the molecules rapidly with the mobile front. In published normal-phase lipid separations on silica and paper, triglycerides routinely migrate in the upper portion of the chromatogram ($R_f = 0.70 - 0.84$).Vitamin E acetate ($\alpha$-tocopheryl acetate) displays even higher mobility. Native unesterified $\alpha$-tocopherol contains a free phenolic hydroxyl group that enables hydrogen bonding and causes it to elute lower down the plate ($R_f \approx 0.45 - 0.55$) in non-polar normal-phase systems. In vitamin E acetate, this phenolic oxygen is masked as an acetate ester.The resulting molecule consists of a bulky chroman ring system substituted with three methyl groups, a single acetate ester, and an aliphatic sixteen-carbon phytyl tail. It possesses a calculated $\log P$ between $10.7$ and $12.2$, zero hydrogen-bond donors, and high lipophilicity. The single ester dipole is sterically shielded by the adjacent methyl substituents on the aromatic ring, minimizing interactions with cellulose hydroxyls. Consequently, the molecule moves with the advancing D-limonene solvent front.The slight separation between vitamin E acetate ($0.84 - 0.92$) and MCT oil ($0.80 - 0.88$) aligns with molecular dipole density: MCT oil presents three exposed ester carbonyl dipoles per molecule, imparting slightly greater polar retention than the single, sterically hindered acetate ester of $\alpha$-tocopheryl acetate.Literature Gaps and Methodological DiscrepanciesA systematic review of historical and contemporary analytical literature reveals four primary evidentiary gaps that must be formally recognized when using this protocol:First, there is a total absence of peer-reviewed planar chromatography data utilizing D-limonene as a primary developing solvent. While limonene has been examined in green analytical chemistry as an extraction solvent to replace hexane and petroleum ethers in Soxhlet procedures, no forensic or academic monographs have standardized planar retention factors on paper or silica using neat terpene carriers. All compendial TLC retention tables published by international bodies (such as UNODC, SWGDRUG, and TIAFT) rely on volatile organic solvents.Second, an evidentiary gap exists regarding the paper chromatography of modern synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists. Forensically significant indazole-3-carboxamides, such as ADB-PINACA and MDMB-4en-PINACA, entered global illicit drug markets between 2012 and 2020—decades after forensic laboratories had phased out paper chromatography in favor of capillary gas chromatography, liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), and automated HPTLC on silica. As a result, published TLC data for these designer compounds is limited to silica gel $60\text{ F}_{254}$ layers developed with ethyl acetate and hexane mixtures; no independent paper chromatography retention values have been cataloged in academic literature.Third, dedicated planar chromatography retention indices for vape cutting agents within cannabis resin matrices are absent from forensic drug analysis manuals. While the analytical chemistry of vitamin E acetate, squalane, and medium-chain triglycerides was heavily investigated following the 2019 outbreak of electronic cigarette-associated lung injury (EVALI), forensic characterization focused on GC-MS, HPLC-UV, and NMR spectroscopy. Planar separation studies for these excipients remain isolated to food-science lipid class fractionation rather than seized-drug screening manuals.Fourth, the physical effect of dissolved curcumin on mobile-phase thermodynamics remains uncharacterized in formal literature. In the Purple Canary formulation, curcumin is dissolved directly into the developing solvent at $0.5\%\text{ w/v}$ rather than sprayed onto the plate post-development. Curcumin is a polyphenolic diferuloylmethane containing phenolic hydroxyls and an active keto-enol tautomeric system. At $0.5\%$ concentration in a non-polar hydrocarbon, curcumin may act as a weak polar mobile-phase modifier, competing for cellulose sorption sites or forming weak intermolecular associations with migrating solutes. DOI: 10.1016/j.jfca.2008.06.012.Chromatographic Synthesis and Presumptive ReliabilityA rigorous cross-check between published forensic data and the empirical findings of the Purple Canary Protocol confirms that the reported migration order is chemically coherent and physically plausible. The relative spatial resolution across the paper strip directly maps to fundamental molecular properties:The strong retention of fentanyl freebase near the origin baseline ($0.12 - 0.18$) reflects the inability of a neutral, non-polar hydrocarbon solvent to overcome the hydrogen-bonding and dipole capture of a basic tertiary amine on a native cellulose-water matrix. Within the natural cannabinoid corridor ($0.34 - 0.58$), the sequence aligns with the interplay between planar aromatic adsorption (retarding CBN) and terpene-solvation homology (accelerating CBD and THC). The synthetic cannabinoids occupy an intermediate-high window ($0.68 - 0.78$), clearly resolved from natural phytocannabinoids due to their lack of phenolic hydroxyl groups, while their internal separation reflects the hydrogen-donating capacity of ADB-PINACA's primary carboxamide versus the non-donating methyl ester of MDMB-4en-PINACA. Finally, the high migration of MCT oil and vitamin E acetate ($0.80 - 0.92$) is fully consistent with the low polar surface area and high lipophilicity characteristic of neutral, ester-capped lipids moving in non-polar developers.Because field paper chromatography operates without climate-controlled environmental chambers or binder-stabilized silica plates, minor retention fluctuations will occur in response to ambient temperature, relative humidity, and chamber vapor equilibration. However, because the relative migration order remains thermodynamically stable, maintaining consistent chamber pre-saturation and pairing the physical run with digital computer-vision densitometry provides a robust, presumptive early-warning analytical method suitable for decentralized harm-reduction screening.
