## Chapter 9: The Purple Canary Protocol (Full SOP)

This chapter provides the complete, unabridged Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for presumptive field screening using the Purple Canary Protocol. It is written to forensic compliance standards, allowing peer specialists, outreach workers, and independent researchers to execute, replicate, and document thin-layer chromatographic analyses with clinical precision.

---

### 1. Document Control & Quality Parameters

* **Procedure Identifier:** SOP-PC-TLC-001


* **Revision:** 2.4 (Open Public Health Edition)


* **Analytical Classification:** Micro-Scale Paper Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC) with Optical Densitometry


* **Scope:** Presumptive qualitative identification of natural phytocannabinoids, synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonists (SCRAs), cutting agents, and anomalous optical adulterants in plant matter, resins, and distillates.


* **Safety Tier:** Low-hazard / Non-carcinogenic (Food-grade mobile phase and developer).



---

### 2. Bill of Materials (BOM) & Reagent Preparation

#### 2.1 Hardware and Reagents

* **Development Chamber:** 350 mL to 500 mL double-walled, vacuum-insulated stainless steel thermal bottle with an airtight threaded cap.


* **Stationary Phase:** Pre-cut Whatman Grade 1 (or equivalent high-purity cellulose) chromatography paper strips, dimensions: $20\text{ mm} \times 80\text{ mm}$.


* **Integrated Developer Formulation:** $95.5\%$ D-Limonene (analytical or food-grade terpene extract, CAS 5989-27-5) to $0.5\%$ Curcumin (diferuloylmethane, standardized turmeric extract, CAS 458-37-7) by weight/volume.


* **Carrier / Extraction Solvent:** Pure D-Limonene or $\ge 91\%$ Isopropyl Alcohol.


* **Micro-Applicators:** Drummond micro-capillary pipettes or fire-polished borosilicate glass capillary tubes ($0.5\ \mu\text{L}$ to $1.0\ \mu\text{L}$ internal volume).


* **Illumination & Optical Sensors:** Long-wave $365\text{ nm}$ ultraviolet (UV-A) torch (filtered black-light LED emitter) and a digital smartphone camera.


* **Accessory Tools:** Nitrile gloves, ceramic extraction well plate (or microcentrifuge tubes), PTFE tweezers, metric stainless steel ruler, and an HB graphite pencil.



#### 2.2 Reagent Preparation & Storage

1. **Developer Storage:** Store the $95.5:0.5$ D-Limonene:Curcumin solution in amber borosilicate dropper bottles away from direct sunlight. The solution remains stable at ambient temperatures ($15^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$) for up to 180 days.


2. **Quality Check (Blank Verification):** Before running suspect samples, verify reagent integrity by developing a blank strip (unspotted paper). Under $365\text{ nm}$ UV, the developed strip should display a uniform, homogeneous golden-yellow fluorescence without dark spots, rings, or premature precipitation lines.

### 3. Step-by-Step Analytical Protocol

```
  PHASE 1: EXTRACTION ──► PHASE 2: SPOTTING ──► PHASE 3: DEVELOPMENT ──► PHASE 4: DIGITIZATION
  (Pinhead sample in      (Capillary touch       (3–5 min migration       (Daylight + UV-A
   carrier solvent)        at 1.5 cm line)        in sealed thermos)       optical capture)

```

#### Phase 1: Micro-Sampling & Dissolution

1. **Sample Mass:** Transfer approximately $1\text{ mg}$ to $3\text{ mg}$ of suspect material into an extraction well. (For dried botanical flower: a crushed particle roughly the size of a pinhead; for distillates: a micro-dab touched to the tip of a clean probe).


2. **Dissolution:** Introduce $2\text{ drops}$ ($\approx 60\ \mu\text{L}$) of carrier solvent. Agitate gently with the applicator tip for 30 seconds until the lipophilic resins and soluble fractions fully disperse.


3. **Sediment Clearance:** Allow particulate plant matter to settle for 20 seconds; sample strictly from the clear liquid supernatant.



#### Phase 2: Stationary Phase Spotting

1. **Baseline Inscription:** Lay a chromatography strip on a clean PTFE or ceramic surface. Using the graphite pencil, lightly draw a horizontal reference line exactly $15\text{ mm}$ ($1.5\text{ cm}$) from the lower edge. Place a faint tick mark at the lateral midpoint. *Under no circumstances use ink or rollerball pens.*


2. **Capillary Loading:** Lower the capillary tube into the extraction liquid. Capillary pressure automatically loads a micro-volume into the bore.


3. **Application:** Lightly touch the capillary tip to the pencil mark on the paper for less than $0.5\text{ seconds}$. Fluid transfers instantly via surface tension.


4. **Boundary Constraint:** Ensure the wet radial spot diameter does not exceed $1.5\text{ mm}$.


5. **Drying Window:** Allow the spot to dry completely for $60\text{ to }90\text{ seconds}$ until no wet carrier ring is perceptible.



#### Phase 3: Chamber Saturation & Development

1. **Chamber Priming:** Dispense $1.5\text{ mL}$ to $2.0\text{ mL}$ of the $95.5:0.5$ developer solution into the bottom reservoir of the thermal bottle. Thread the cap tightly and allow the interior headspace to equilibrate with vapor for $120\text{ seconds}$.


2. **Insertion:** Unscrew the cap. Using PTFE tweezers, grasp the top edge of the dried test strip and lower it vertically into the bottle until the bottom edge rests evenly on the chamber floor. Verify visually that the liquid meniscus is below the $1.5\text{ cm}$ graphite origin line.


3. **Incubation:** Seal the cap securely to maintain internal vapor pressure. Maintain the chamber undisturbed on a level surface.


4. **Development Timing:** Allow migration to proceed for exactly $3\text{ to }5\text{ minutes}$. Capillary draw pulls the terpene front upward at approximately $10\text{ mm to }15\text{ mm per minute}$.


5. **Termination:** Open the chamber, remove the strip with tweezers, and immediately mark the highest reach of the liquid (the solvent front) with a faint pencil stroke. Allow the strip to dry in ambient air for $60\text{ seconds}$.



#### Phase 4: Optical Inspection & Densitometry

1. **Substrate Alignment:** Lay the developed strip flat on a clean, non-reflective matte white background.


2. **Daylight Exposure (Pass 1):** Capture a high-resolution, perpendicular image under even, diffuse ambient lighting ($4000\text{K to }5000\text{K}$ CRI preferred). This records physical strip geometry, verifies baseline integrity, and maps natural botanical pigments.


3. **UV-A Luminescence (Pass 2):** Darken ambient room lighting. Position the $365\text{ nm}$ UV torch at a $45^\circ$ angle relative to the strip at a fixed distance of $6\text{ cm to }8\text{ cm}$. Capture the secondary exposure, capturing the excited curcumin fluorescence matrix and optical quenching bands.

### 4. Mathematical Analysis & Peak Calculation

The exact position of every analyte band is quantified as a standardized Retention Factor ($R_f$):

$$R_f = \frac{d_{\text{analyte}}}{d_{\text{solvent}}}$$

Where:

* $d_{\text{analyte}}$ is the vertical distance measured from the pencil origin line ($R_f = 0.00$) to the center of density (centroid) of the target band.


* $d_{\text{solvent}}$ is the total vertical distance from the pencil origin line to the pencil-marked solvent front line ($R_f = 1.00$).



```
  Pencil Solvent Front ───────────────────────────── Rf = 1.00  (d_solvent)
                             ▲
                             │
                       [ Band Centroid ] ─────────── Rf = d_analyte / d_solvent
                             │
  Pencil Origin Baseline ─── ● ───────────────────── Rf = 0.00

```

### 6. Environmental Constraints & Troubleshooting

Chromatographic behavior is governed by thermodynamic and atmospheric conditions. The following matrix details common anomalies and mandated procedural corrections:

| Failure Mode | Root Cause | Observable Artifact | Mandatory Remediation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Crescent / Skewed Front**<br> | Strip touching chamber interior wall during climb.

 | Solvent front curves unevenly at margins.

 | Ensure vertical centering; strip must hang suspended without contact.

 |
| **Baseline Drag / Streaking**<br> | Overloaded sample mass ($>3\text{ mg}$) or wet insertion.

 | Continuous dark tail running from origin to front.

 | Discard strip. Dilute sample in solvent ($3\times$), allow full $90\text{ s}$ dry before run.

 |
| **Premature Run Stalling**<br> | Chamber cap cross-threaded; vapor loss to room.

 | Migration stalls below $40\text{ mm}$ at $5\text{ minutes}$.

 | Re-seat chamber gasket, ensure tight seal during saturation cycle.

 |
| **Haloing / Ring Bleed**<br> | Sample spotted using water or high-surface-tension base.

 | Sample forms a ring at the perimeter of the dot.

 | Ensure extraction uses pure D-Limonene or $\ge 91\%$ Isopropanol only.

 |
| **Washout Failure**<br> | Chamber overfilled ($>2.5\text{ mL}$); meniscus touches baseline.

 | Target sample leaches down into bottom solvent pool.

 | Discard strip and pool. Re-fill with exactly $1.5\text{ mL}$; origin line must clear liquid.

 |

### 7. Documentation & Archival Logging

Every test executed under this protocol must be transcribed into an archival log (physical ledger or encrypted digital entry) containing the following mandatory fields:

* **Log ID & Date/Timestamp**

* **Operator Identification**

* **Sample Matrix:** (e.g., Loose botanical flower, vape distillate, counterfeit tablet)


* **Development Metrics:** Migration distance ($d_{\text{solvent}}$ in mm), total migration time (seconds)


* **Calculated Peaks:** List of observed $R_f$ centroids and corresponding optical behaviors (Fluorescent / Quenched / Colorimetric)


* **Presumptive Verdict:** (Clean Botanical Profile / Adulterant Flagged / Inconclusive — Re-test Advised)


* **Digital Capture Hash:** File verification identifier of associated daylight and UV photographic passes



---

> **Operational Standard Note:** This SOP governs presumptive screening only. When anomalous bands or high-hazard quenching flags occur, the sample must be designated as presumptive positive for contamination and diverted from human consumption. Formal confirmation requires laboratory GC-MS or HPLC evaluation.
